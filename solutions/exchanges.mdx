Run a trading product without building custody, an order book or blockchain infrastructure. You own the interface, the customer and the pricing; Quidax provides execution and settlement underneath.

## What you need

| Component | Product |
| --- | --- |
| Customer accounts and custody | [Wallets](/wallets) sub-accounts |
| Deposits and withdrawals | [Wallets](/wallets) addresses and withdrawals |
| Execution | [Order Book](/wallets/trading) |
| Market data | [Order Book](/wallets/trading#market-data) |
| Local currency funding | [Ramp](/ramp) |
| Simple conversions | [Swap](/wallets/swap) |

## Build it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Accounts per user">
    `POST /users` with your internal ID in `reference`. Deposits then attribute automatically rather than needing manual reconciliation.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Deposits">
    Generate addresses lazily, with an explicit `network`.

    **Credit at `ACCEPTED` only**, after re-querying. A deposit confirmed on-chain can still be held or fail AML screening.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Prices">
    `GET /markets/tickers` for the board, `GET /markets/tickers/{market}` for one pair.

    Use tickers for display. Order book and depth responses are far heavier — reach for them for a trading view or slippage calculation, not a headline price.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Orders">
    `POST /users/{user_id}/orders`.

    **Round to the pair's precision and check the minimum** — the equivalent of $1 — before submitting. These two rules cause most rejections.

    **Expect** `order.done`. An order can fill across several trades — reconcile against `GET /users/{user_id}/trades`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Withdrawals">
    Pre-generated unique `reference`, explicit `network`, and resolution by reference for anything uncertain.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Limit or market orders

| Type | Behaviour | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limit | Rests on the book at your price | The user set a price and can wait |
| Market | Fills immediately at best available | The user wants certainty of execution |

<Warning>
  A market order carries no price guarantee. On a thin pair it can fill materially away from the last traded price. If you quote a rate to a user before placing one, you carry the difference.
</Warning>

For a plain "convert X to Y" experience, [Swap](/wallets/swap) is the better tool — quote, show, confirm, with no partial fills.

## What will bite you

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Precision and minimums" icon="ruler">
    Each pair caps decimal places on volume and price independently. Round in your own code rather than passing raw user input through — a rejection after the user has confirmed is a much worse experience than a rounded amount shown up front.

    <Card title="Precision by pair" icon="ruler" href="/wallets/conventions#precision" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Balance fields" icon="scale-balanced">
    Spendable balance is `balance` alone. `locked` is committed to open orders and pending withdrawals — showing it as available lets users place orders that cannot fill.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Market ordering is not stable" icon="list">
    `GET /markets` is sorted by Quidax's internal ranking, not alphabetically, and the order can change. Do not rely on position.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Trade-only assets" icon="ban">
    Several listed assets can be bought and sold but never sent, received, or given an address. Never offer a deposit flow for one.

    <Card title="Coverage and limits" icon="globe" href="/wallets/coverage" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Rate limits" icon="gauge-high">
    300 requests per minute, 20 per second on address generation. Polling tickers per user per second will exhaust that quickly — poll once centrally and fan out internally.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Custody is regulated" icon="scale-balanced">
    Holding customer funds is a licensed activity in most markets. Confirm your position during onboarding before you build.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
