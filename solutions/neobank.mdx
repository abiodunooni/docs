Your customers already have a local-currency account with you. Adding a dollar balance they can fund, hold and spend from is usually the highest-value thing you can build next in a market with a volatile currency.

## What you add

| Feature | Product |
| --- | --- |
| A dollar balance per customer | [Wallets](/wallets) sub-accounts |
| Fund it from local currency | [Ramp](/ramp) on-ramp |
| Withdraw back to local currency | [Ramp](/ramp) off-ramp |
| Receive crypto from outside | [Wallets](/wallets) addresses |
| Send to anyone | [Wallets](/wallets) withdrawals |
| Convert between currencies | [Swap](/wallets/swap) |

One account, one secret key, across all of it.

## Build it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Give every customer a sub-account">
    `POST /users` with your internal user ID in `reference`.

    Balances are then held and tracked per customer, and `reference` comes back on wallet and transaction payloads so you do not need a mapping table.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Let them fund from local currency">
    Ramp on-ramp, settled into that customer's sub-account.

    **Credit only on `buy_transaction.successful` with `data.status: completed`.** A transaction in `processing` has not settled.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Show the balance in both currencies">
    `GET /users/{user_id}/wallets/usdt`

    `balance` is the dollar figure. `converted_balance` expresses it in `reference_currency` — which is what most customers actually look at.

    **Never add `locked` or `staked`** to the spendable figure.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Let them receive from outside">
    Generate an address on demand, with an explicit `network`.

    **Credit at `ACCEPTED` only**, after re-querying the deposit.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Let them send and withdraw">
    Wallets withdrawals for crypto, Ramp off-ramp for local currency into a bank account.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Decisions to make early

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Custody is regulated" icon="scale-balanced">
    Holding customer funds is a licensed activity in most markets. Settle this before writing code — confirm your position during onboarding.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Who verifies the customer" icon="id-card">
    You already onboard customers for the banking product. Whether that verification satisfies the crypto legs depends on your licence.

    The Ramp **widget** has Quidax verify each user inside the flow, which is the faster route if your licence does not cover it. The **API** has you verify under your own licence.

    <Card title="Widget or API" icon="split" href="/ramp#widget-or-api" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="One ledger or two" icon="book">
    Quidax holds the crypto balances; your core banking system holds the fiat. Your product needs a view across both, and a reconciliation job that runs on a schedule rather than at month end.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Limits will shape onboarding" icon="gauge-high">
    Limits are per customer per rolling 24 hours and rise with the verification a customer has completed. If your customers fund regularly, surface the tier upgrade path in-product rather than letting them hit a wall.

    <Card title="Coverage and limits" icon="globe" href="/ramp/coverage" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which assets to expose" icon="list-check">
    Most neobank customers want one stablecoin, not a list of thirty assets. Several supported assets are trade-only and cannot be sent or received at all.

    <Card title="Coverage and limits" icon="globe" href="/wallets/coverage" horizontal />
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
