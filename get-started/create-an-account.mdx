Create a Quidax business account to begin onboarding and access the developer environment.

Each platform has its own account, credentials and webhook settings. Sign up on the platform for the product you are building with.

| Product | Sign up |
| --- | --- |
| Payments | [dashboard.basqet.com/signup](https://dashboard.basqet.com/signup) |
| Ramp | [ramp-merchant.quidax.io/sign-up](https://ramp-merchant.quidax.io/sign-up) |
| Wallets and Orderbook | [pro.quidax.io/en_US/register](https://pro.quidax.io/en_US/register) — select the **Business** tab |

## Before you begin

You should have:

- Your official business name.
- A business email address controlled by your organisation.
- A phone number.
- Basic information about your organisation.
- Access to an authenticator application, such as Google Authenticator.

## Account ownership

The Wallets and Orderbook platform does not have a team feature yet, so dashboard access sits with one account. Two models work well — pick the one that matches how your team operates.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Single owner" icon="user">
    One person creates and manages the account, and is the only one who signs in.

    Best where one engineer or operator runs the integration day to day, and dashboard actions are infrequent.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Shared account" icon="users">
    The account is registered on a shared, organisation-controlled email address that several people can access.

    Best where more than one person needs to create keys, check balances or process withdrawals.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Note>
  Whichever model you choose, hold the Google Authenticator second factor with someone senior who is reliably reachable. Dashboard withdrawals and API key creation both need a code, so availability of that person is what keeps your operations moving.
</Note>

Practical steps that make either model safer:

- Register the account on a mailbox your organisation controls, not an individual's inbox.
- Store the password and the authenticator recovery details in your team's password manager.
- Make sure at least two people can restore the second factor if a device is lost.
- Agree in advance who approves and executes withdrawals, and document it. See [Segregation of duties](#segregation-of-duties).
- Review who has access whenever someone joins or leaves the team.

## Two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication protects your dashboard and the actions that move funds.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install an authenticator app">
    Google Authenticator is supported across all platforms.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Scan the QR code">
    Open the security settings in your dashboard and scan the code shown.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm the code">
    Enter the six-digit code to complete setup.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Store the recovery details">
    Keep the recovery information in your organisation's password manager so more than one person can restore access.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  On the Wallets and Orderbook platform, two-factor authentication is checked when you create API keys and when you process withdrawals. Setting it up early means both are ready when you need them.
</Note>

## Segregation of duties

The Wallets and Orderbook platform has no built-in dual approval. Dashboard actions are taken from a single login, so the platform cannot enforce a four-eyes check on a withdrawal on its own.

You can still build a maker-checker flow around the second factor. A dashboard withdrawal needs two things, and they do not have to sit with the same person: access to the dashboard, and a current six-digit code from the authenticator app.

| Role | Holds | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maker** | The dashboard password | Signs in, prepares the withdrawal, enters the destination and the amount |
| **Checker** | The authenticator device | Reviews what has been prepared, and supplies the code only if it is correct |

Separating the two means no single person can move funds out unaided.

<Warning>
  This is a procedural control, not an enforced one. The platform records the action against one account, so its logs cannot evidence that two people were involved. Keep your own record of who initiated and who approved each withdrawal if you need to demonstrate the split to an auditor.
</Warning>

If your control framework requires enforced dual approval, raise it during onboarding. Until multi-user access ships, [transaction limits](#transaction-limits) and a deliberately small group of people with dashboard access are the controls available alongside a maker-checker split.

## Transaction limits

Quidax can configure transaction limits on your account as an additional control on the value moving through the dashboard and the API.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum transaction amount | Caps the value of any single transaction |
| Cumulative daily limit | Caps the total value processed in a day |

A transaction that would breach a configured limit is rejected rather than queued.

## Notifications and reporting

Quidax sends an email when a transaction is performed on your account, so activity is visible without anyone signing in to check. Notifications go to the registered account email address.

| You want | Use |
| --- | --- |
| To be told when something happens on the account | Transaction emails |
| Notification inside your own systems | [Webhooks](/wallets/webhooks) |
| Scheduled reporting or reconciliation | The [deposit](/wallets/deposits) and [withdrawal](/wallets/withdrawals) history endpoints |

## What is not available yet

The Wallets and Orderbook platform is a single-login product today. The consequences are worth designing around before you go live.

| Not available | What it means | Work around it with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multiple users | One set of credentials covers everyone who needs access | An [ownership model](#account-ownership) agreed up front |
| Roles and permissions | You cannot separate the person who needs API keys from the person who processes withdrawals | Limiting who holds the password and the authenticator |
| Per-user activity attribution | Dashboard records show the account, not the individual | Your own record of who did what |
| Enforced dual approval | The platform does not require a second person to approve a payment | A [maker-checker split](#segregation-of-duties) |

<Note>
  Multi-user access, with team permissions and per-user activity, is on the roadmap. Your account team can tell you where it currently sits.
</Note>

## Account security

To protect your integration:

- Use an email address controlled by your organisation.
- Enable two-factor authentication.
- Do not share account passwords outside your password manager.
- Create separate API credentials for each environment and platform.
- Restrict credentials to authorised systems and team members.
- Never place secret keys in frontend or mobile application code.
- Rotate credentials when access changes or exposure is suspected.
