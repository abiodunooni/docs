Create a Quidax business account to begin onboarding and access the developer environment.

Each platform has its own account, credentials and webhook settings. Sign up on the platform for the product you are building with.

| Product | Sign up |
| --- | --- |
| Payments | [dashboard.basqet.com/signup](https://dashboard.basqet.com/signup) |
| Ramp | [ramp-merchant.quidax.io/sign-up](https://ramp-merchant.quidax.io/sign-up) |
| Wallets and Orderbook | [pro.quidax.io/en_US/register](https://pro.quidax.io/en_US/register) — select the **Business** tab |

## Before you begin

You should have:

- Your official business name.
- A business email address controlled by your organisation.
- A phone number.
- Basic information about your organisation.
- Access to an authenticator application, such as Google Authenticator.

## Account ownership

The Wallets and Orderbook platform does not have a team feature yet, so dashboard access sits with one account. Two models work well — pick the one that matches how your team operates.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Single owner" icon="user">
    One person creates and manages the account, and is the only one who signs in.

    Best where one engineer or operator runs the integration day to day, and dashboard actions are infrequent.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Shared account" icon="users">
    The account is registered on a shared, organisation-controlled email address that several people can access.

    Best where more than one person needs to create keys, check balances or process withdrawals.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Note>
  Whichever model you choose, hold the Google Authenticator second factor with someone senior who is reliably reachable. Dashboard withdrawals and API key creation both need a code, so availability of that person is what keeps your operations moving.
</Note>

Practical steps that make either model safer:

- Register the account on a mailbox your organisation controls, not an individual's inbox.
- Store the password and the authenticator recovery details in your team's password manager.
- Make sure at least two people can restore the second factor if a device is lost.
- Agree in advance who approves and executes withdrawals, and document it.
- Review who has access whenever someone joins or leaves the team.

## Two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication protects your dashboard and the actions that move funds.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install an authenticator app">
    Google Authenticator is supported across all platforms.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Scan the QR code">
    Open the security settings in your dashboard and scan the code shown.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm the code">
    Enter the six-digit code to complete setup.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Store the recovery details">
    Keep the recovery information in your organisation's password manager so more than one person can restore access.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  On the Wallets and Orderbook platform, two-factor authentication is checked when you create API keys and when you process withdrawals. Setting it up early means both are ready when you need them.
</Note>

## Account security

To protect your integration:

- Use an email address controlled by your organisation.
- Enable two-factor authentication.
- Do not share account passwords outside your password manager.
- Create separate API credentials for each environment and platform.
- Restrict credentials to authorised systems and team members.
- Never place secret keys in frontend or mobile application code.
- Rotate credentials when access changes or exposure is suspected.
