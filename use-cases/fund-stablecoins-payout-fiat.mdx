You hold stablecoin. Your recipients need naira, cedis or mobile money. Ramp converts and delivers into local accounts, so you never hold local currency between funding and payout.

This is the shape of most remittance, marketplace payout and treasury-distribution products in Africa.

**Product:** [Ramp](/ramp) off-ramp — stablecoin in, local currency out.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the transaction">
    Create an off-ramp transaction with the customer's details and the amount.

    **You get back** a reference and a quoted local-currency amount.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Attach the payout account">
    Add the recipient's bank or mobile money account.

    **Expect** a name check. `Name does not match` means the resolved account name does not match the customer on the transaction — surface it as a name problem, not a generic failure.

    Bank codes must come from the institutions endpoint, not a list you maintain.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm to get a deposit address">
    **You get back** a deposit address and the exact amount to send.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Send the exact amount">
    Send exactly what was quoted, on the right network. A different asset, a different amount or the wrong network pushes the transaction into `needs_attention` for manual review.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Watch it settle">
    **Expect** `sell_transaction.successful`. NGN bank payouts typically settle in under two minutes, GHS mobile money in under five.

    Reconcile against the payout status rather than assuming acceptance is final — a provider can accept and later reverse.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Practical considerations

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Accounts must belong to the transacting party" icon="id-card">
    Payouts go to accounts belonging to the person on the transaction. Design your beneficiary collection around this from the start — retrofitting it after launch means re-collecting details from every user.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Mobile money versus bank rails" icon="mobile">
    In Ghana, mobile money reaches most consumer endpoints and settles in under five minutes. Bank rails reach formal account holders, settle more slowly and carry higher per-transaction caps.

    Consumer products usually lead with mobile money; higher-ticket and B2B flows need bank rails.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A payout can be reversed after acceptance" icon="rotate-left">
    Uncommon, but it happens. Watch for `sell_transaction.failed` and have a process for providing an alternative account.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Limits are per customer, per rolling day" icon="gauge-high">
    Not per merchant, and not per calendar day — a customer who transacts at 15:00 regains that headroom at 15:00 the next day.

    There is no endpoint for remaining headroom, so track consumption yourself if you want to warn users before Ramp rejects them.

    <Card title="Coverage and limits" icon="globe" href="/ramp/coverage" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Multi-corridor treasury" icon="globe">
    Each corridor settles in its own local currency to a local account. Aggregating across currencies would place FX risk on Quidax on your behalf.

    For a single consolidated position, settle to stablecoin and convert on your own treasury side.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
