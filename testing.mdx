## Testing Payments in the sandbox

Sandbox credentials are issued as soon as you create your Basqet account, so you can integrate while your business review is in progress.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use sandbox keys">
    Keep sandbox and live credentials in separate environment variables, and make the active environment visible in your logs and admin tools.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Exercise the payment lifecycle">
    Create payment requests and drive them through each status your application handles.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Point webhooks at a test endpoint">
    Register a sandbox webhook URL and confirm your handler verifies, acknowledges and processes events idempotently.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Repeat once on live keys">
    Before launch, run one small live payment end to end to confirm production credentials, webhooks and settlement.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Testing Ramp, Wallets and Orderbook in production

These products are tested against production, with real balances and real settlement. Treat the first phase of testing as a controlled pilot.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fund a small operational balance">
    Use an amount large enough to cover minimums and network fees, and small enough to be inconsequential.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Restrict access">
    Limit the integration to internal users or a feature flag until the flow is verified.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Run one transaction per flow">
    Complete a full cycle for each flow you support.

    - **Ramp** — one on-ramp and one off-ramp, in each currency you are approved for.
    - **Wallets** — create a subaccount, generate an address, receive a deposit, send a withdrawal.
    - **Orderbook** — place a market order, place and cancel a limit order, run a conversion.

    <Note>
      On Wallets and Orderbook, withdrawals are confirmed with your Google Authenticator code. Have the authenticator to hand, and make sure whoever holds it is available for your first withdrawal test.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Reconcile every test transaction">
    Confirm each transaction appears with matching references, amounts and fees in both Quidax and your own system.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Test the unhappy paths deliberately">
    Trigger validation errors, insufficient balances, unsupported networks and limit breaches with intentionally invalid requests. These cost nothing and are where most launch issues hide.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Scale up gradually">
    Increase volume in steps, monitoring errors, latency and reconciliation at each level.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Tag every test transaction with a recognisable internal reference prefix. It makes reconciliation trivial and keeps test records easy to exclude from reporting.
</Tip>

## Scenarios to cover

Do not test only the successful path. Your integration should handle:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Transaction states" icon="arrows-rotate">
    - Successful transactions.
    - Pending transactions.
    - Failed transactions.
    - Expired transactions.
    - Reversed or refunded transactions.

    The full list of statuses for each flow is on the product's pages.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Webhooks" icon="webhook">
    - Duplicate webhook delivery.
    - Delayed webhook delivery.
    - Out-of-order events.
    - Webhook received before your own record is committed.
    - Your endpoint being briefly unavailable.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Inputs and limits" icon="triangle-alert">
    - Invalid input.
    - Insufficient balances.
    - Unsupported currencies or networks.
    - Transaction-limit errors.
    - Amounts below the minimum or above the maximum.

    Error codes, response statuses and limits are documented per product.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Network conditions" icon="wifi">
    - Network timeouts.
    - Rate limiting, and how your client backs off.
    - Retries that must not create duplicate transactions.
    - Requests where the response is lost but the transaction succeeded.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
