Two official packages cover the two halves of an integration: one for your server, one for your checkout page.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="basqet-node" icon="server" href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/payments-node">
    Server-side library. Initialize transactions, fetch currencies and verify payments from your backend.
  </Card>

  <Card title="basqet-checkout" icon="window" href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/payments-checkout">
    Client-side library. Embed the payment modal in your site so customers pay without leaving checkout.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Both are typed, and each ships its own installation and usage documentation on npm.

## Which to use

| | Checkout SDK | Direct API |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who builds the payment screen | Basqet | You |
| Currency picker, address, QR code | Handled for you | You render them |
| Effort | Embed the modal | Two REST calls plus your own interface |
| Control over the experience | Standard checkout | Complete |

<Tip>
  Use the checkout modal unless you have a specific reason not to. It handles the currency picker, the address display, the QR code and the countdown — all the parts customers get wrong — and you still receive the same webhooks.
</Tip>

## How the modal fits together

<Steps>
  <Step title="Initialize on your server">
    Call `POST /v1/transaction` from your backend with the amount and the customer's email. Never do this from the browser, since it would expose your keys.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open the modal">
    Pass the transaction reference to `basqet-checkout` on the client. The customer picks a currency and pays inside the modal.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Act on the webhook">
    Give value when `payment.received` arrives on your server. The modal closing is not confirmation that the customer paid.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Your secret key belongs on the server only. The public key is the one that can safely reach the browser.
</Warning>
