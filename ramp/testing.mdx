Ramp is tested against production, with real balances and real settlement. That means what you verify is exactly what your customers will experience — no sandbox drift, no surprises at launch.

<Note>
  A sandbox is on the roadmap. Until it lands, test with the minimums below, which keep a full end-to-end run trivially cheap.
</Note>

## Test amounts

| Direction | Currency | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On-ramp | NGN | ₦3,000 |
| On-ramp | GHS | GHS 50 |
| Off-ramp | USDT | 5 USDT |
| Off-ramp | USDC | 5 USDC |

A complete on-ramp and off-ramp round trip costs a few dollars in fees and spread.

## Run a controlled pilot

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fund a small operational balance">
    Enough to cover the minimums plus network fees, and small enough to be inconsequential.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Restrict access">
    Keep the integration behind a feature flag or limited to internal users until every flow is verified.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Tag every test transaction">
    Use a recognisable `merchant_reference` prefix, for example `test-`. Reconciliation becomes trivial and test records stay easy to exclude from reporting.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Run one transaction per flow">
    Complete a full cycle for each direction and each currency you are approved for.

    - **On-ramp, NGN** — create, confirm, pay the exact amount, confirm the stablecoin arrives.
    - **On-ramp, GHS** — cover both `next_step` values, `PIN` and `OTP`.
    - **Off-ramp** — create, fetch institutions, attach the payout account, confirm, send, confirm the payout lands.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Reconcile each one">
    Confirm every transaction appears with matching references, amounts and fees in both Ramp and your own system. Check `processor_fee`, `vat`, `blockchain_fee` and `stamp_charge` land where you expect.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Scale up gradually">
    Raise volume in steps, watching errors, latency and reconciliation at each level.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Test the failures deliberately

Most launch problems hide in paths nobody exercised. These cost nothing, because the request is rejected before money moves.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Validation errors" icon="triangle-alert">
    - An amount below the minimum, and one above the maximum.
    - A malformed wallet address.
    - A network the asset does not support.
    - A reused `merchant_reference`.
    - A missing customer first or last name.
    - A request with no `x-private-key` header, and one with a wrong key.

    Confirm your client surfaces each message usefully rather than showing a generic failure.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Name mismatch" icon="user-xmark">
    Attach an off-ramp payout account whose registered name differs from the customer on the transaction, and confirm you get `Name does not match` and present it as a name problem.

    On on-ramp, this path costs a real transaction to reproduce, since the deposit is rejected and refunded. Handle it in code and confirm the refund flow works rather than forcing it repeatedly.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Wrong payment amount" icon="equals">
    Confirm your interface shows `amount_expected` and not `amount`, and that a customer cannot proceed with a rounded figure. This single mistake causes more failed on-ramps than anything else.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Expired quotes" icon="clock">
    Let a transaction sit for more than 10 seconds before confirming, and check your refresh path recalculates correctly and shows the customer the new figure.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Mobile money branches" icon="mobile">
    Test both `PIN` and `OTP`. Submit a wrong OTP, submit an expired one, and re-initiate to trigger a resend. Confirm submitting the same OTP twice does not double-charge.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Test your webhook handler

You do not need a live transaction for most of this. Replay captured payloads against your endpoint.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Signature verification" icon="key">
    - A valid signature passes.
    - A tampered body fails.
    - A missing `X-Ramp-Signature` header is rejected.
    - Verification runs against the raw body, not re-serialised JSON. Add a test that would fail if middleware reordered keys.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Delivery behaviour" icon="webhook">
    - The same event delivered twice results in one credit.
    - A `processing` event arriving after `successful` does not move the transaction backwards.
    - Your endpoint returns `2xx` within the 10-second timeout while doing real work asynchronously.
    - An event arriving before your own record exists is retried, not discarded.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Recovery" icon="wifi">
    Take your endpoint down, run a transaction, bring it back, and confirm your reconciliation job picks up the missed event by listing transactions over a date range.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Refunds" icon="rotate-left">
    Replay `buy_transaction.refund.details_requested`, confirm it reaches an operations queue, and verify a repeat of the same event for one `refund_reference` does not submit details twice.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Before you launch

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Credentials" icon="key">
    - The secret key lives server-side only and is not in any client bundle.
    - Only the public key appears in browser or mobile code.
    - Keys are in environment variables, not source control.
    - You know who rotates keys and how.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Operations" icon="gauge-high">
    - Both references are stored against every order and are searchable by support.
    - Failed and `needs_attention` transactions raise an alert rather than sitting silently.
    - Someone owns the refund queue.
    - Your team knows to contact support with references rather than retrying manually.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Customer experience" icon="users">
    - The exact payment amount is unmissable in your interface.
    - Customers are told to pay from an account in their own name.
    - Limit and validation errors are shown in language a customer understands.
    - Off-ramp customers are told the exact amount, asset, network and tag.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
