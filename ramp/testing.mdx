Ramp is tested against production, with real balances and real settlement. That means what you verify is exactly what your customers will experience — no sandbox drift, no surprises at launch.

<Note>
  A sandbox is on the roadmap. Until it lands, test with the minimums below, which keep a full end-to-end run trivially cheap.
</Note>

## Test amounts

| Direction | Currency | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On-ramp | NGN | ₦3,000 |
| On-ramp | GHS | GHS 50 |
| Off-ramp | USDT | 5 USDT |
| Off-ramp | USDC | 5 USDC |

A complete on-ramp and off-ramp round trip costs a few dollars in fees and spread.

## Before you start

<Steps>
  <Step title="Generate a production API key">
    In the merchant dashboard, go to **Settings > API Keys & Webhooks** and select **Generate new API keys**. Store the secret key server-side only.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure your webhook endpoint">
    Set a publicly reachable HTTPS URL in the dashboard. Your handler should verify `X-Ramp-Signature`, store the event, return `2xx` promptly and process idempotently.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Fund a small operational balance">
    Enough to cover the minimums plus network fees, and small enough to be inconsequential.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Restrict access and tag your transactions">
    Keep the integration behind a feature flag or internal users. Use a recognisable `merchant_reference` prefix such as `test-`, so test records are trivial to find and exclude from reporting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The controlled production test

Run this sequence end to end before opening the flow to any real cohort. Use the smallest amount your production limits permit.

| # | Action | Pass criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Access and setup | The key is stored server-side and the webhook endpoint is reachable. |
| 1 | Create the transaction | `200` with `status: ok`. The transaction is `pending`. The `TRX-...` reference and `to_amount` are stored separately from your `merchant_reference`. |
| 2 | Refresh, if the amount changes | Updated `from_amount` and `to_amount` return against the same `merchant_reference`. No duplicate transaction is created. |
| 3 | Confirm | A bank account and `amount_expected` return, with the fee and VAT fields. |
| 4 | Present payment instructions | The amount displayed equals `amount_expected` exactly. The base `amount` is never shown as the transfer figure when the two differ. |
| 5 | Pay from a name-matched account | Ramp confirms the deposit and the transaction moves to `processing`. |
| 6 | Handle the processing event | The handler accepts repeated `processing` events safely. **The customer is not credited at this stage.** |
| 7 | Verify settlement | `buy_transaction.successful` arrives with `data.status: completed`. The customer is credited exactly once and the destination balance increases. |
| 8 | Reconcile across systems | Amounts, currency, `merchant_reference` and state match across the API response, the webhook, the dashboard, the balance and your own ledger. |
| 9 | Validate failure handling | `data.status: needs_attention` and `error_message` are recorded, the customer is not credited, and your escalation path fires. |
| 10 | Open limited traffic | Only after every check above passes. Monitor errors, latency, webhook delivery, balances and reconciliation before increasing. |

<Warning>
  **Stop conditions.** Do not open public traffic if any of these is true:

  - The production API key is unavailable or not working.
  - The first transaction does not settle to its destination.
  - The `successful` webhook is not received.
  - The dashboard and your ledger do not reconcile.
  - You cannot guarantee that a customer is credited exactly once.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Do not deliberately force a live failure to test step 9 without agreeing it with the Quidax team first. Use sample payloads replayed against your own endpoint instead.
</Note>

## Cover every flow you support

Repeat the sequence for each direction and currency you are approved for.

- **On-ramp, NGN** — create, confirm, pay the exact amount, confirm the asset arrives.
- **On-ramp, GHS** — cover both `next_step` values, `PIN` and `OTP`.
- **On-ramp, custodial mode** — omit `wallet_address` and confirm the asset lands in your merchant wallet rather than on-chain.
- **Off-ramp** — create, fetch institutions, attach the payout account, confirm, send, confirm the payout lands.
- **Custodial withdrawal** — if you withdraw from the merchant wallet, send a small amount on-chain, confirm the total debit is `amount + fee`, and confirm `custodial_withdraw.success` arrives with a `txid`.

Check that `processor_fee`, `vat`, `blockchain_fee` and `stamp_charge` all land where you expect.

## Test the failures deliberately

Most launch problems hide in paths nobody exercised. These cost nothing, because the request is rejected before money moves.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Validation errors" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    - An amount below the minimum, and one above the maximum.
    - A malformed wallet address.
    - A network the asset does not support.
    - A reused `merchant_reference`.
    - A missing customer first or last name.
    - A request with no `x-private-key` header, and one with a wrong key.

    Confirm your client surfaces each message usefully rather than showing a generic failure.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Name mismatch" icon="user-xmark">
    Attach an off-ramp payout account whose registered name differs from the customer on the transaction, and confirm you get `Name does not match` and present it as a name problem.

    On on-ramp, this path costs a real transaction to reproduce, since the deposit is rejected and refunded. Handle it in code and confirm the refund flow works rather than forcing it repeatedly.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Wrong payment amount" icon="equals">
    Confirm your interface shows `amount_expected` and not `amount`, and that a customer cannot proceed with a rounded figure. This single mistake causes more failed on-ramps than anything else.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Expired quotes" icon="clock">
    Let a transaction sit for more than 10 seconds before confirming, and check your refresh path recalculates correctly and shows the customer the new figure.

    Then let one sit longer and fund it anyway. Ramp re-quotes at the rate current on arrival rather than rejecting the deposit, so confirm your ledger records the settled amount and not the figure you last displayed.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Withdrawal idempotency" icon="rotate">
    Submit the same `merchant_reference` twice with an identical body and confirm you get the original withdrawal back rather than a second transfer. Then submit it with a different amount and confirm you get `409` and handle it as a conflict, not as a retryable error.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Mobile money branches" icon="mobile">
    Test both `PIN` and `OTP`. Submit a wrong OTP, submit an expired one, and re-initiate to trigger a resend. Confirm submitting the same OTP twice does not double-charge.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Test your webhook handler

You do not need a live transaction for most of this. Replay captured payloads against your endpoint.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Signature verification" icon="key">
    - A valid signature passes.
    - A tampered body fails.
    - A missing `X-Ramp-Signature` header is rejected.
    - Verification runs against the raw body, not re-serialised JSON. Add a test that would fail if middleware reordered keys.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Delivery behaviour" icon="webhook">
    - The same event delivered twice results in one credit.
    - A `processing` event arriving after `successful` does not move the transaction backwards.
    - Your endpoint returns `2xx` within the 10-second timeout while doing real work asynchronously.
    - An event arriving before your own record exists is retried, not discarded.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Recovery" icon="wifi">
    Take your endpoint down, run a transaction, bring it back, and confirm your reconciliation job picks up the missed event by listing transactions over a date range.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Refunds" icon="rotate-left">
    Replay `buy_transaction.refund.details_requested`, confirm it reaches an operations queue, and verify a repeat of the same event for one `refund_reference` does not submit details twice.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Before you launch

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Credentials" icon="key">
    - The secret key lives server-side only and is not in any client bundle.
    - Only the public key appears in browser or mobile code.
    - Keys are in environment variables, not source control.
    - You know who rotates keys and how.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Operations" icon="gauge-high">
    - Both references are stored against every order and are searchable by support.
    - Failed and `needs_attention` transactions raise an alert rather than sitting silently.
    - Someone owns the refund queue.
    - Your team knows to contact support with references rather than retrying manually.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Customer experience" icon="users">
    - The exact payment amount is unmissable in your interface.
    - Customers are told to pay from an account in their own name.
    - Limit and validation errors are shown in language a customer understands.
    - Off-ramp customers are told the exact amount, asset, network and tag.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Acceptance criteria

Treat launch as blocked until all of these hold:

- The production API key is active and stored securely.
- Your HTTPS webhook endpoint receives and safely processes all required events.
- One controlled transaction has completed end to end.
- That transaction settled to its destination and appears in **Transaction Monitoring**.
- Your customer was credited exactly once, and every reference and amount reconciles.
- Named technical contacts, a support channel, pause authority and an initial traffic cap are agreed.

<Tip>
  That last one is the one teams skip. Agree in advance **who can pause the flow** and what the initial traffic cap is, on both sides. It costs a five-minute conversation before launch and saves an hour of confusion during an incident.
</Tip>
