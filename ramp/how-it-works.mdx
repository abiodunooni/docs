Every Ramp transaction has two legs: a fiat leg and a crypto leg. Ramp converts between them and settles both. The direction determines which leg comes first.

| Direction | Also called | Customer sends | Customer receives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| On-ramp | Buy | Local currency | Stablecoin |
| Off-ramp | Sell | Stablecoin | Local currency |

## On-ramp lifecycle

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="API">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Create the transaction">
        Send the amount, currency pair, customer details and the destination wallet address. You get back a quote and a Quidax `reference`.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Refresh if the quote goes stale">
        Quotes are held for roughly 10 seconds. If your customer takes longer to decide, refresh to recalculate. A deposit that lands after expiry is re-quoted at the rate current on arrival.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Confirm the transaction">
        This generates a single-use bank account and returns `amount_expected`, the exact total to pay. Confirm only when the customer is ready.
      </Step>

      <Step title="The customer pays">
        They transfer `amount_expected` from an account in their own name.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Ramp converts and pays out">
        You receive `buy_transaction.processing` when the deposit lands, and again when the payout starts.
      </Step>

      <Step title="The transaction completes">
        `buy_transaction.successful` arrives with the transaction hash. Requery if you need to confirm.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Card title="On-ramp guide" icon="arrow-down" href="/ramp/on-ramp" horizontal>
      Full request and response detail, including mobile money.
    </Card>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Widget">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Your customer opens the widget">
        They enter the amount they want to buy.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Quidax verifies them, once">
        A first-time user verifies their email by OTP and completes identity verification inline — an independent check run on your behalf. It happens on their first transaction only; after that they are recognised and go straight to payment.
      </Step>

      <Step title="A bank account is generated">
        The widget shows the account and the exact amount.
      </Step>

      <Step title="They pay, and receive stablecoin">
        Your `onSuccess` callback fires with the transaction details.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

</Tabs>

## Off-ramp lifecycle

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="API">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Create the transaction">
        Send the asset amount, network, target fiat currency and customer details.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Fetch supported institutions">
        Pull the live list of banks and mobile money providers for the country.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Attach the payout account">
        Ramp resolves the account and checks the name against the customer on the transaction.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Confirm the transaction">
        This returns the deposit address. Confirm only after the payout account is attached.
      </Step>

      <Step title="The customer sends the asset">
        Exact amount, correct asset, correct network.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Ramp converts and pays out">
        `sell_transaction.processing` fires when the deposit is accepted and again when the payout starts, then `sell_transaction.successful` on completion.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Card title="Off-ramp guide" icon="arrow-up" href="/ramp/off-ramp" horizontal />
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Widget">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Your customer opens the widget">
        They enter the amount they want to sell.
      </Step>

      <Step title="They enter their payout account">
        The bank or mobile money account that will receive the funds.
      </Step>

      <Step title="A deposit address is generated">
        Your `onReceiveWalletDetails` callback fires with the address, network and amount.
      </Step>

      <Step title="They send, and receive local currency">
        Ramp pays out directly to their account.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Transaction statuses

`status` on a transaction moves through these values.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `pending` | Created, but the customer has not paid or sent funds yet. |
| `processing` | Funds received. Conversion or payout is under way. |
| `completed` | Both legs finished. This is the terminal success state. |
| `failed` | The transaction could not be completed. |
| `needs_attention` | One leg succeeded and the other did not. Quidax reviews these manually. |
| `refunded` | Funds were returned to the customer. |
| `abandoned` | Created but never funded within the window — 36 minutes on on-ramp, 24 hours on off-ramp. |
| `cancelled` | Cancelled before completion. |
| `swap_reversed` | A conversion was reversed. |

<Note>
  Treat webhooks as the source of truth for the final state, and requery the transaction when you need to confirm. Both are covered in [Webhooks](/ramp/webhooks).
</Note>

## Rules that apply to every transaction

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="References" icon="hashtag">
    There are two references and they are not interchangeable.

    - `merchant_reference` is the unique reference **you** generate. Use it to create, refresh, confirm and attach accounts, and to look up refunds.
    - `reference` is the reference **Quidax** returns, formatted `TRX-...`. Use it to fetch a transaction.

    Store both against your own order record. Reusing a `merchant_reference` returns a duplicate transaction error.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Exact amounts" icon="equals">
    On on-ramp, the customer must transfer exactly `amount_expected`. That figure is the transaction amount plus the processor fee and VAT. Overpayment and underpayment are both rejected by the provider, so show the exact figure and discourage rounding.

    On off-ramp, the customer must send the exact asset amount on the correct network. A different amount can push the transaction into `needs_attention` for manual review.

    <Warning>
      A rejected on-ramp payment produces **no webhook**. See [When a payment is rejected](#when-a-payment-is-rejected).
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Transaction windows" icon="clock">
    A transaction that is never funded is abandoned automatically:

    - **On-ramp** — 36 minutes after creation.
    - **Off-ramp** — 24 hours after creation.

    Size your payment screens accordingly. On on-ramp in particular, 36 minutes is short enough that a customer who wanders off mid-payment will come back to a dead account number, so confirm only when they are ready to pay.

    Abandonment does not emit a webhook. Poll the transaction, or reconcile with the list endpoints, if you need to know.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Name matching, and who counts as the customer" icon="user-check">
    The fiat account on a transaction must belong to the **customer named on that transaction**. Ramp checks the paying account on on-ramp and the payout account on off-ramp.

    Where a mismatch is found, the fiat deposit is rejected and refunded, or the payout account is not attached.

    **This does not mean the sender and the beneficiary have to be the same person.** The customer on a transaction is whoever owns the fiat account on that leg, and you name them when you create it. So a remittance paying out to a recipient works normally: you initiate the off-ramp naming the *recipient* as the customer, with the recipient's account, and the check passes.

    In other words, third-party pay-ins and payouts are a supported pattern on the API. What is not supported is a fiat account belonging to someone other than the customer you named.

    <Note>
      This works on the API because **you** verified those customers. Passing your own customer's name carries the obligation to have verified them and to produce that verification on request. On the widget the position is different — Quidax verifies the end user inside the flow, so the customer is always a Quidax-verified user.
    </Note>

    For business customers, pass the first word of the business name as `first_name` and the remainder as `last_name`.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Quotes and rates" icon="clock">
    A quote is held for roughly 10 seconds once a transaction is initiated. Refresh the transaction to recalculate if your customer takes longer.

    Quoted rates include the transaction fee and any markup you have configured, and `to_amount` is net of the Ramp fee. Stamp duty, where it applies, is deducted from the converted amount afterwards and is not in the quote.

    **If funds arrive after the quote has expired**, Ramp does not reject the deposit or hold it. It re-quotes at the rate current when the deposit lands and settles on that figure. Ramp does not absorb rate movement — whoever commits a number to the customer does.

    Quotes are also one-directional: you price from the amount being sold, and Ramp returns what the customer receives. There is no way to name a target payout amount and have Ramp derive the input. See [Guaranteeing a fiat amount](/ramp/rates-and-markup#guaranteeing-a-fiat-amount-to-your-customer).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Virtual accounts" icon="building-columns">
    On-ramp uses a dynamic virtual account by default: a fresh account number per transaction, so no payment reference is needed and your customer does not have to add a narration.

    Duplicate deposits to the same account are rejected and reversed by the provider.

    Static accounts, where one customer keeps the same account number across transactions, are available for NGN on request. See [auto swap](/ramp/on-ramp#recurring-funding-with-auto-swap).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Settlement timing" icon="stopwatch">
    On-ramp fiat is typically confirmed in under 30 seconds, with the stablecoin send bound by the network's block time. Off-ramp payouts are typically under 2 minutes for NGN and under 5 minutes for GHS mobile money. Crypto deposits on off-ramp are typically detected in under a minute.

    NGN payouts have no customer-facing cutoff window — they are processed in real time, including at weekends and outside banking hours. If NIBSS or the receiving bank is unavailable, Ramp retries once the service is back, with no action needed from you.

    <Card title="Payout timing and cutoffs" icon="stopwatch" href="/ramp/off-ramp#payout-timing-and-cutoffs" horizontal />
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## When a payment is rejected

This is the one behaviour worth reading twice, because it generates more support traffic than anything else in Ramp.

If a customer pays the **wrong amount** into the virtual account, the payment provider rejects and reverses the transfer *before it reaches Ramp*. Quidax never sees the money. The consequence:

- **No webhook fires.** Not `processing`, not `failed`, nothing.
- **The transaction does not change.** It stays `pending`.
- **After 36 minutes it abandons**, silently.
- **The provider returns the money to the customer**, on the payment rail's own timeline.

So your customer will tell you they paid while your system shows nothing at all. That is expected behaviour, not an incident, and no amount of waiting will produce an event.

<Warning>
  Silence after a payment is the signature of a wrong-amount transfer. Before escalating, check the amount the customer actually sent against `amount_expected`.
</Warning>

**What to do.** Confirm the amount they transferred, tell them the reversal is coming from the provider rather than from you, and create a fresh transaction for the correct amount. The old one abandons on its own.

**How to avoid it.** Display `amount_expected` prominently, never `amount`, and discourage rounding. Most occurrences are a customer rounding ₦5,080.63 down to ₦5,000.

A **name mismatch** behaves differently. That deposit does reach Ramp, which is why it produces a refund and a `refund.details_requested` webhook rather than silence. See [Refunds](/ramp/refunds).

## Fees

Fees appear on the transaction so you can reconcile against them.

| Field | Applies to | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `processor_fee` | Both | The payment or blockchain processor's fee. |
| `vat` | On-ramp confirm | VAT charged on the processor fee. |
| `blockchain_fee` | On-ramp | Network fee for the stablecoin send. |
| `stamp_charge` | Off-ramp | Stamp duty, where the payout meets the local threshold. |

<Note>
  In Nigeria, stamp duty of ₦50 applies to payouts of ₦10,000 or more. It is deducted from the converted amount after conversion, so it is not included in the quote.
</Note>

Ramp is priced as a percentage transaction fee on the principal, applied inside the quoted rate. You can add your own markup on top of it, set in the dashboard per market and per direction, and withdraw what accrues.
