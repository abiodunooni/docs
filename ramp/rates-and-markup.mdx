description: "How Ramp prices a transaction, and how to add your own markup to earn revenue on it."

Ramp lets you add your own percentage markup on top of the quoted rate. It is collected on every transaction and accrues as revenue you withdraw from the merchant dashboard — which is how wallet providers, aggregators and platforms earn on the flow rather than just carrying the cost of it.

## How a rate is built

<Steps>
  <Step title="Quidax quotes the base rate">
    The live rate for the currency pair, sourced from Quidax liquidity.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The Quidax transaction fee is applied">
    A percentage of the principal. This is your cost of using Ramp.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Your markup is applied">
    The percentage you configure, on top of everything above. This is your revenue.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The customer sees one rate">
    Quotes and `to_amount` come back with all of the above already baked in, so your customer sees a single number.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Provider fees and stamp duty sit outside the rate. Processor fees appear as their own fields on the transaction, and Nigerian stamp duty is deducted after conversion. See [Fees](#fees-outside-the-rate) below.
</Note>

## Configure your markup

Markup is configured in the **merchant dashboard**. There is no API for it.

<Note>
  This is deliberate. Pricing is a commercial setting rather than a runtime one, so it changes in one place under whatever approval your team requires — not from application code where a deploy could move your margin unnoticed.
</Note>

You can set a different percentage for each combination of:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Market" icon="globe">
    Price NGN and GHS independently, based on what each market bears.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Direction" icon="arrow-right-arrow-left">
    Price on-ramp and off-ramp separately. Most merchants do, because the competitive pressure differs on each side.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Tip>
  Start conservative and adjust. Because the markup lives inside the quoted rate, a change takes effect on the next quote — you never ship code to move your pricing.
</Tip>

## How you get paid

Your markup accrues per transaction as a balance on your merchant account. **You withdraw it from the dashboard** when you want it.

Nothing to invoice and nothing to reconcile against your customers: the difference between the base rate and what your customer paid is already yours, and it simply accumulates until you draw it down.

Because the quote already includes your markup, `to_amount` is exactly what your customer receives. You do not deduct anything yourself before displaying it.

<Note>
  Markup revenue is separate from the funds moving through a transaction. A withdrawal draws down accrued markup, not customer balances.
</Note>

## Worked example

A customer sells 10 USDT for NGN. The rate you receive from the quote endpoint already carries the transaction fee and your markup:

```
Quoted rate                   1,378.46 NGN/USDT
10 USDT × 1,378.46          = 13,784.60 NGN
Stamp duty (payout ≥ ₦10,000) −    50.00 NGN
                            = 13,734.60 NGN credited to the customer
```

Your markup is inside the `1,378.46`, so it needs no separate handling in your integration. Stamp duty is the only deduction applied after conversion.

<Warning>
  If you show customers a net figure before they commit, subtract stamp duty yourself on NGN payouts of ₦10,000 or more. It is not in the quote.
</Warning>

## Fees outside the rate

These appear as their own fields on the transaction, so you can reconcile against each one.

| Field | Applies to | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `processor_fee` | Both | The payment or blockchain processor's fee. |
| `vat` | On-ramp confirm | VAT charged on the processor fee. |
| `blockchain_fee` | On-ramp | Network fee for the stablecoin send. |
| `stamp_charge` | Off-ramp | Stamp duty, where the payout meets the local threshold. |

On on-ramp, the processor fee and VAT are added on top of the transaction amount. `amount_expected` on the confirm response is the total the customer transfers, and it is the figure to display.

## Quote validity

A quote is held for roughly 10 seconds once a transaction is initiated. If your customer takes longer to proceed, refresh the transaction to recalculate rather than confirming against a stale rate.

<CodeGroup>
```bash On-ramp quote
curl 'https://ramp-be.quidax.io/api/v1/merchants/purchase_quotes/buy?currency=ngn&token=usdt&fiat_amount=20000&token_network=bep20' \
  -H "x-private-key: $RAMP_SECRET_KEY"
```

```bash Off-ramp quote
curl 'https://ramp-be.quidax.io/api/v1/merchants/purchase_quotes/sell?token=usdt&currency=ngn&token_amount=10&token_network=bep20' \
  -H "x-private-key: $RAMP_SECRET_KEY"
```
</CodeGroup>

```json Response
{
  "status": "ok",
  "message": "Fiat currency amount retrieved",
  "data": { "fee": "50.0", "to_amount": "15947.94" }
}
```

Quotes fetched this way are indicative. The binding rate is the one returned when you create or refresh a transaction.

<Note>
  **The fee is already deducted.** `to_amount` on a quote is net of the Ramp fee — the `fee` field tells you what came out, it is not a charge to add on top. Show `to_amount` as the figure your customer receives.
</Note>

### What happens when a quote expires

The 10-second window governs the *quoted* rate, not the transaction. A deposit that arrives after the quote it was priced against has expired is neither rejected nor held:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ramp re-quotes at the rate current when the deposit arrives">
    The transaction is repriced against the live rate at that moment.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The transaction settles on the new rate">
    Your customer receives what the re-quoted rate produces, which may be more or less than the original figure.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  This is the behaviour to design around if you show your customer a figure before they act. Ramp does not absorb rate movement between the quote and the deposit — whoever commits to the customer absorbs it. See [Guaranteeing a fiat amount](#guaranteeing-a-fiat-amount-to-your-customer).
</Warning>

## Guaranteeing a fiat amount to your customer

A common pattern, especially in remittance and payout flows, prices the transaction in the **fiat the recipient receives**:

1. You quote the customer a receive amount in NGN.
2. The customer sends USDC on-chain.
3. You instruct a payout of the NGN amount quoted in step 1.

Step 2 depends on a person acting, so no 10-second window can hold across all three. The workable design is to **guarantee your customer the fiat figure and take whatever crypto amount Ramp needs at settlement** — you absorb the rate movement, and Ramp never has to commit to a rate it cannot hold.

<Warning>
  **Ramp cannot derive the token amount from a target fiat amount.** The sell quote endpoint takes `token_amount` and returns `to_amount` in fiat. There is no reverse direction: you cannot specify the NGN you want paid out and have Ramp compute the USDC required. Off-ramp transactions are created the same way, from the asset amount.
</Warning>

So the derivation is yours to do. The shape that works:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Quote the customer from your own pricing">
    Your pricing engine produces the NGN figure you commit to, using your own rate and margin. This is the number the customer sees and the only one you guarantee.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Derive the token amount at settlement, not at quote time">
    When the deposit lands, fetch a sell quote for a nominal `token_amount` to read the effective rate, divide your committed NGN by it, then fetch a second quote for the token amount you calculated and check its `to_amount` covers your commitment. Fees and stamp duty do not scale with the amount, so verify against the second quote rather than trusting the arithmetic.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create the off-ramp transaction for that token amount">
    Ramp converts and pays out. Your customer receives the amount you promised.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Book the difference">
    The gap between the crypto the customer sent and the crypto the payout consumed is your spread on that transaction — positive or negative. It belongs in your own P&L, not in reconciliation against Ramp.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Quote and settle in the same operation, close together. The exposure you are carrying runs from the moment you commit a figure to your customer until the deposit lands, so a re-quote at settlement keeps it as short as it can be.
</Note>

Two consequences worth planning for:

- **Price your buffer deliberately.** You are short the rate between quote and settlement. Size the margin your pricing engine adds to cover normal movement over that window, rather than discovering it on volatile days.
- **Decide what happens on a large adverse move.** If the crypto received no longer covers the NGN you quoted, you either pay the shortfall or go back to the customer. Pick one before it happens, and put a threshold on it.

## Layering your own revenue model

You can take margin on Ramp in two ways, and they combine.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Configured markup" icon="percent">
    A percentage you set in the dashboard, priced inside the quoted rate. Ramp collects it on every transaction and it accrues as a balance you withdraw.

    Simplest to run: one number across the quote, the transaction and your revenue, and nothing to reconcile.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Your own pricing on top" icon="calculator">
    Treat the Ramp rate as wholesale, quote your customer from your own pricing engine, and keep the difference.

    More work, but it is what a flow like the fiat-guaranteed pattern above requires, because your quote has to exist before Ramp prices anything.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Tip>
  If you run your own pricing engine, you can still configure a markup — but pick one place for your margin. Margin in both means your effective spread is the product of two numbers that change independently, and nobody can tell you what it is on a given day.
</Tip>

## Building for it

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Prefer a configured markup to margin in your own code" icon="calculator">
    Where your product can quote the customer from a live Ramp rate, configure the markup in the dashboard. Adding margin in application code means the amount you quote and the amount Ramp settles diverge, and every reconciliation has to account for the gap. A configured markup keeps one number across the quote, the transaction and your revenue.

    The exception is a flow that has to commit a fiat figure before Ramp prices anything, like [guaranteeing a fiat amount](#guaranteeing-a-fiat-amount-to-your-customer). There the pricing has to be yours — but keep the margin in one place rather than splitting it across both.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Quote close to the moment of commitment" icon="clock">
    Fetch the quote when the customer is ready to act, not when they open the screen. A quote fetched early is usually stale by the time they confirm.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Handle re-quotes gracefully" icon="arrows-rotate">
    If a refresh returns a materially different rate, show the customer the new figure and ask them to confirm again rather than proceeding silently on the old one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reconcile markup separately from fees" icon="chart-line">
    Markup accrues to a balance you draw down from the dashboard, while `processor_fee`, `vat`, `blockchain_fee` and `stamp_charge` are per-transaction costs on the transaction itself. Track them apart so your margin reporting stays clean.

    Because withdrawals are periodic and manual, your accrued markup will not line up with any single day's transactions. Reconcile the accrual against transaction volume, and the withdrawal against your bank separately.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Decide who owns the pricing" icon="users">
    Since markup is dashboard-only, a change is a commercial decision rather than a deploy. Agree who is allowed to make it and who reviews it, in the same way you would for any other pricing change.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
