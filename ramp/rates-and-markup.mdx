Ramp lets you add your own percentage markup on top of the quoted rate. It is collected on every transaction and accrues as revenue you withdraw from the merchant dashboard — which is how wallet providers, aggregators and platforms earn on the flow rather than just carrying the cost of it.

## How a rate is built

<Steps>
  <Step title="Quidax quotes the base rate">
    The live rate for the currency pair, sourced from Quidax liquidity.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The Quidax transaction fee is applied">
    A percentage of the principal. This is your cost of using Ramp.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Your markup is applied">
    The percentage you configure, on top of everything above. This is your revenue.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The customer sees one rate">
    Quotes and `to_amount` come back with all of the above already baked in, so your customer sees a single number.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Provider fees and stamp duty sit outside the rate. Processor fees appear as their own fields on the transaction, and Nigerian stamp duty is deducted after conversion. See [Fees](#fees-outside-the-rate) below.
</Note>

## Configure your markup

Markup is configured in the **merchant dashboard**. There is no API for it.

<Note>
  This is deliberate. Pricing is a commercial setting rather than a runtime one, so it changes in one place under whatever approval your team requires — not from application code where a deploy could move your margin unnoticed.
</Note>

You can set a different percentage for each combination of:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Market" icon="globe">
    Price NGN and GHS independently, based on what each market bears.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Direction" icon="arrow-right-arrow-left">
    Price on-ramp and off-ramp separately. Most merchants do, because the competitive pressure differs on each side.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Tip>
  Start conservative and adjust. Because the markup lives inside the quoted rate, a change takes effect on the next quote — you never ship code to move your pricing.
</Tip>

## How you get paid

Your markup accrues per transaction as a balance on your merchant account. **You withdraw it from the dashboard** when you want it.

Nothing to invoice and nothing to reconcile against your customers: the difference between the base rate and what your customer paid is already yours, and it simply accumulates until you draw it down.

Because the quote already includes your markup, `to_amount` is exactly what your customer receives. You do not deduct anything yourself before displaying it.

<Note>
  Markup revenue is separate from the funds moving through a transaction. A withdrawal draws down accrued markup, not customer balances.
</Note>

## Worked example

A customer sells 10 USDT for NGN. The rate you receive from the quote endpoint already carries the transaction fee and your markup:

```
Quoted rate                   1,378.46 NGN/USDT
10 USDT × 1,378.46          = 13,784.60 NGN
Stamp duty (payout ≥ ₦10,000) −    50.00 NGN
                            = 13,734.60 NGN credited to the customer
```

Your markup is inside the `1,378.46`, so it needs no separate handling in your integration. Stamp duty is the only deduction applied after conversion.

<Warning>
  If you show customers a net figure before they commit, subtract stamp duty yourself on NGN payouts of ₦10,000 or more. It is not in the quote.
</Warning>

## Fees outside the rate

These appear as their own fields on the transaction, so you can reconcile against each one.

| Field | Applies to | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `processor_fee` | Both | The payment or blockchain processor's fee. |
| `vat` | On-ramp confirm | VAT charged on the processor fee. |
| `blockchain_fee` | On-ramp | Network fee for the stablecoin send. |
| `stamp_charge` | Off-ramp | Stamp duty, where the payout meets the local threshold. |

On on-ramp, the processor fee and VAT are added on top of the transaction amount. `amount_expected` on the confirm response is the total the customer transfers, and it is the figure to display.

## Quote validity

A quote is held for roughly 10 seconds once a transaction is initiated. If your customer takes longer to proceed, refresh the transaction to recalculate rather than confirming against a stale rate.

<CodeGroup>
```bash On-ramp quote
curl 'https://ramp-be.quidax.io/api/v1/merchants/purchase_quotes/buy?currency=ngn&token=usdt&fiat_amount=20000&token_network=bep20' \
  -H "x-private-key: $RAMP_SECRET_KEY"
```

```bash Off-ramp quote
curl 'https://ramp-be.quidax.io/api/v1/merchants/purchase_quotes/sell?token=usdt&currency=ngn&token_amount=10&token_network=bep20' \
  -H "x-private-key: $RAMP_SECRET_KEY"
```
</CodeGroup>

```json Response
{
  "status": "ok",
  "message": "Fiat currency amount retrieved",
  "data": { "fee": "50.0", "to_amount": "15947.94" }
}
```

Quotes fetched this way are indicative. The binding rate is the one returned when you create or refresh a transaction.

<Note>
  During periods of currency volatility the quote window still applies. If a customer does not complete payment inside it, Ramp re-quotes rather than absorbing the movement.
</Note>

## Building for it

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do not add your margin in your own code" icon="calculator">
    Configure it in the dashboard instead. Adding a margin client-side means the amount you quote and the amount Ramp settles diverge, and every reconciliation has to account for the gap. A configured markup keeps one number across the quote, the transaction and your revenue.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Quote close to the moment of commitment" icon="clock">
    Fetch the quote when the customer is ready to act, not when they open the screen. A quote fetched early is usually stale by the time they confirm.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Handle re-quotes gracefully" icon="arrows-rotate">
    If a refresh returns a materially different rate, show the customer the new figure and ask them to confirm again rather than proceeding silently on the old one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reconcile markup separately from fees" icon="chart-line">
    Markup accrues to a balance you draw down from the dashboard, while `processor_fee`, `vat`, `blockchain_fee` and `stamp_charge` are per-transaction costs on the transaction itself. Track them apart so your margin reporting stays clean.

    Because withdrawals are periodic and manual, your accrued markup will not line up with any single day's transactions. Reconcile the accrual against transaction volume, and the withdrawal against your bank separately.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Decide who owns the pricing" icon="users">
    Since markup is dashboard-only, a change is a commercial decision rather than a deploy. Agree who is allowed to make it and who reviews it, in the same way you would for any other pricing change.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
