## Payment methods

Methods differ by currency and by channel. Call [List payment methods](/api-reference/ramp/rates-and-limits/list-payment-methods) for the live set rather than assuming.

| Currency | On-ramp | Off-ramp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NGN | Virtual bank account, dynamic by default, static on request | Bank transfer |
| GHS | Mobile money | Mobile money and bank transfer |

In Ghana, mobile money reaches most consumer endpoints and settles in under five minutes. Bank rails reach formal account holders, settle more slowly and carry higher per-transaction caps. Consumer products usually lead with mobile money; higher-ticket and B2B flows often need bank rails.

<Tip>
  For predictable settlement timing, default to Tron or Polygon and use Ethereum only when the destination wallet requires it. Gas spikes on Ethereum mainnet delay sends by minutes, and Ramp queues rather than overpaying gas.
</Tip>

## Transaction tiers

Customer limits apply **per customer, per day** — the ₦5,000,000 NGN buy cap and the 7,000 USDC sell cap are each what a single end customer can transact in a day, not a pool shared across your customer base. One customer reaching their cap does not consume headroom for the rest. The tier a customer sits in is determined by how much verification that customer has completed.

Caps are enforced per corridor, so reaching a cap in one market does not affect another. On off-ramp the per-customer cap is 7,000 USDC per day; call [Get off-ramp limits](/api-reference/ramp/rates-and-limits/get-off-ramp-limits) for the live figure on each asset.

The **API** tabs below describe account-level tiers for the business transacting on its own account. Those sit alongside the per-customer caps rather than replacing them, so check both when sizing throughput.

### What "daily" means

`daily` is a **rolling 24-hour window**, not a calendar day. There is no fixed reset time and no timezone to reason about.

A customer who buys ₦5,000,000 at 15:00 has no headroom left until 15:00 the following day, when that spend ages out of the window. Because the window rolls continuously, headroom returns transaction by transaction as each one passes the 24-hour mark, rather than all at once at midnight.

### Tracking consumption

There is no endpoint that returns a customer's consumed or remaining limit. [Get on-ramp limits](/api-reference/ramp/rates-and-limits/get-on-ramp-limits) and [Get off-ramp limits](/api-reference/ramp/rates-and-limits/get-off-ramp-limits) return the cap itself, not how much of it is left.

If you want to show a customer their remaining headroom, or block them before Ramp rejects them, track their confirmed transactions on your side over the same rolling 24-hour window.

### Handling a limit breach

A transaction that would exceed a limit is rejected before any money moves. Ramp never partially fills. Surface the message to your customer and, where you can, offer a smaller amount.

Because the window rolls, a customer who is blocked now may have headroom again within hours without doing anything. If you tell them when to come back, base it on 24 hours from their earlier transactions, not on midnight.

## Settlement times

| Direction | Rail | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On-ramp | Bank transfer or virtual account | Under 5 minutes |
| Off-ramp | NGN bank payout | Under 2 minutes |
| Off-ramp | GHS mobile money | Under 5 minutes |

Times assume the local rail is available. Nigeria's instant rail runs continuously; some bank rails have processing hours, and transactions submitted outside them are queued.
