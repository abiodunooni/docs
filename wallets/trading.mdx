Buy and sell on behalf of your users against Quidax's order book, and read the market data behind it.

<Note>
  For a simple "convert X to Y" experience, use [Swap](/wallets/swap) instead. Orders are the right tool when you want control over price and execution; swap is the right tool when you just want the conversion done.
</Note>

## Order types

| Type | Behaviour | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Limit** | Sits on the order book at your price. Executes when the market reaches it. | You care about the price and can wait. |
| **Market** | Executes immediately at the best available price. | You care about certainty of execution, not price. |

<Warning>
  A market order gives no price guarantee. On a thin pair it can fill materially away from the last traded price — if you quote your customer a rate before placing one, you carry the difference.
</Warning>

## Orders

| Action | Method | Path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create an order | `POST` | `/users/{user_id}/orders` |
| Fetch all orders | `GET` | `/users/{user_id}/orders` |
| Fetch one order | `GET` | `/users/{user_id}/orders/{order_id}` |
| Cancel an order | `POST` | `/users/{user_id}/orders/{order_id}/cancel` |

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `WAIT` | Queued for execution. |
| `DONE` | Executed. |
| `CANCEL` | Cancelled. |

Only limit orders can meaningfully be cancelled — a market order executes immediately.

## Minimums and precision

Two rules reject orders more often than anything else.

**Minimum order value** — the equivalent of **$1**, in whichever currency the pair is quoted in. Because it is a dollar equivalent rather than a fixed per-currency figure, compute it from a live rate rather than hard-coding one.

**Precision.** Each pair caps the decimal places allowed for volume and price. For `BTC/USDT`, BTC volume takes 5 decimals, USDT volume 2, and price 2. Submitting more precision fails.

<Card title="Precision by pair" icon="ruler" href="/wallets/conventions#precision" horizontal>
  The full table, and why you should not treat it as permanent.
</Card>

<Tip>
  Round to the pair's precision in your own code before submitting rather than passing a user's raw input through. A rejected order after a customer has confirmed is a much worse experience than a rounded amount shown up front.
</Tip>

## Market data

| Action | Method | Path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List all markets | `GET` | `/markets` |
| Markets summary | `GET` | `/markets/summary` |
| List market tickers | `GET` | `/markets/tickers` |
| One market's ticker | `GET` | `/markets/tickers/{market}` |
| Order book for a market | `GET` | `/markets/{currency}/order_book` |
| Depth data | `GET` | `/markets/{currency}/depth` |
| K-line (OHLC) data | `GET` | `/markets/{market}/k` |
| K-line with pending trades | `GET` | `/markets/{currency}/k_with_pending_trades/{trade_id}` |

A market is a pair like `btcusdt` or `usdtngn`. The list endpoint is sorted by Quidax's internal ranking of markets, so the order is not alphabetical and is not stable — do not rely on position.

<Tip>
  Ticker data is the right source for displaying a price. Order book and depth are for building a trading interface or calculating slippage — they are considerably heavier responses, so do not poll them to show a headline price.
</Tip>

## Trades

| Action | Method | Path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your executed trades | `GET` | `/users/{user_id}/trades` |
| Recent trades for a market | `GET` | `/trades/{market}` |

Your own trades are what you reconcile against — an order can fill in several trades, so a `DONE` order may correspond to more than one.

## Order events

| Event | Fires when |
| --- | --- |
| `order.done` | An order executed. |
| `order.cancelled` | An order was cancelled. |
