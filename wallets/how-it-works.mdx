Wallets gives you an account tree. Your merchant account sits at the top, each of your customers gets a sub-account beneath it, and every account holds a wallet per currency. Money enters through a blockchain address, moves between accounts internally, and leaves on-chain or to a bank.

## The account model

```
Your merchant account  (me)
├── Sub-account            → wallets → addresses
├── Sub-account            → wallets → addresses
└── Sub-account            → wallets → addresses
```

Every endpoint takes a `user_id` that decides which account you are acting on: `me` for the parent, or a sub-account's ID.

<Warning>
  One secret key reaches every account in the tree, including withdrawals. There is no per-sub-account credential — protect the key accordingly.
</Warning>

## Receiving money

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the sub-account">
    `POST /users` with the customer's email and your own `reference`.

    **You get back** an `id`. That is the `user_id` for every later call. Store it against your user.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check the currency can be received">
    `GET /users/{user_id}/wallets/{currency}`

    **You get back** `blockchain_enabled`, `default_network` and a `networks` array. Each network carries `deposits_enabled` independently.

    **Stop here** if `blockchain_enabled` is false or no network has `deposits_enabled: true`. Some assets are trade-only and can never receive funds.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Generate an address">
    `POST /users/{user_id}/wallets/{currency}/addresses?network=trc20`

    **Always pass `network` explicitly.** Omitting it uses the currency default, which for a multi-network asset is rarely what your customer is sending from.

    **You get back** an address — or you may not. Generation is asynchronous for some currencies.

    **Expect** a `wallet.address.generated` webhook when it is ready. Listen for it rather than polling.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Show the address">
    Display the address, the network, and the `destination_tag` if one is present. A chain like XRP needs both — an address without its tag credits the wrong account.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Wait for the deposit">
    **Expect** `deposit.transaction.confirmation` as confirmations arrive, then `deposit.successful` once confirmed.

    **Do not credit yet.**
  </Step>

  <Step title="Re-query and credit">
    `GET /users/{user_id}/deposits/{deposit_id}`

    **Credit only when `status` is `ACCEPTED`.** A deposit that has been seen on-chain can still be held, rejected, or fail AML screening.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  The gap between `deposit.successful` and `ACCEPTED` is where most integration bugs live. Crediting on the webhook alone means crediting money that can still be reversed by a compliance hold.
</Warning>

## Sending money

Withdrawals all use one endpoint. What you put in `fund_uid` decides the rail.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the balance">
    `GET /users/{user_id}/wallets/{currency}`

    **Spendable balance is `balance` alone.** `locked` is committed to open orders and pending withdrawals; `staked` is unavailable. Adding either will oversell the position.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check the network allows withdrawals">
    From the same response, find your network in `networks` and confirm `withdraws_enabled` is true. A network can accept deposits while withdrawals are disabled.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Generate and store a reference">
    Before you call. If the request times out you will need it to find out what happened.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create the withdrawal">
    `POST /users/{user_id}/withdraws`

    | `fund_uid` | Rail |
    | --- | --- |
    | Blockchain address | On-chain |
    | Account ID | Internal, instant, no fee |

    **Naira payouts are not available here.** They are dashboard-only — use [Ramp off-ramp](/ramp/off-ramp) for programmatic local currency.

    **You get back** a withdrawal with `status: processing`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Watch for the outcome">
    **Expect** `withdraw.successful` or `withdraw.rejected`.

    A rejected withdrawal carries a `reason`. Read it before retrying — most rejections will repeat.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Reconcile">
    `GET /users/{user_id}/withdraws/reference/{reference}`

    Compare `amount`, `fee` and `total` against your own ledger. `total` is what actually left the wallet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  There is a 6-second cancellation window after a withdrawal is created, and no address-validation endpoint. Once that window closes, an on-chain withdrawal to a wrong address is gone permanently.
</Warning>

## Moving money internally

Set `fund_uid` to a destination **account ID** rather than a blockchain address. No chain is touched, so it settles instantly and costs nothing.

This is how you sweep customer balances into your treasury account. Do not route these through an on-chain withdrawal — you would pay a network fee to move money that never needed to leave Quidax.

## Converting

| You want | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A simple "convert X to Y" | [Swap](/wallets/swap) — quote, show, confirm |
| Control over price and execution | [Trading](/wallets/trading) — limit and market orders |

Swap quotes and order fills both change balances, and both emit `wallet.updated`.

## Statuses at a glance

| Resource | States |
| --- | --- |
| Deposit | `SUBMITTING` → `SUBMITTED` → `CHECKED` → **`ACCEPTED`**, or `ON_HOLD` / `REJECTED` / `FAILED` / `FAILED_AML` |
| Withdrawal | `PROCESSING` → `DONE`, or `REJECTED` |
| Order | `WAIT` → `DONE`, or `CANCEL` |

<Note>
  Statuses are uppercase in these tables and lowercase in webhook payloads. Compare case-insensitively.
</Note>
