There is no sandbox. Wallets is tested against production, with real balances and real blockchain transactions. What you verify is exactly what your customers will experience — no sandbox drift, no surprises at launch.

<Note>
  Keep test amounts tiny. A full round trip — deposit, sweep, withdraw — costs only the network fees on the chain you pick.
</Note>

## Choosing test amounts

Pick the cheapest chain you support, not the one your customers will use most.

| Chain | Why |
| --- | --- |
| TRC-20 (Tron) | Cheapest for USDT. Best default for testing. |
| BEP-20 (BSC) | Cheap, and covers most listed assets. |
| Bitcoin, Ethereum | Avoid for routine testing. Fees dwarf the test amount. |

Internal transfers between your own accounts are **free and instant**, so exercise that path as much as you can before spending anything on-chain.

<Warning>
  Minimum order values still apply when you test trading: ₦1,000 or $0.90. Below those, orders are rejected.
</Warning>

## Before you start

<Steps>
  <Step title="Generate a production API key">
    In the dashboard, open **API management** and create a key. Restrict it to your server's IP address. Store it server-side only.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure your webhook endpoint">
    A publicly reachable HTTPS URL, with your signing secret set. Your handler should verify `quidax-signature`, store the event, return `200` promptly and process idempotently.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Fund a small operational balance">
    Enough to cover a few test transactions and network fees, small enough to be inconsequential if something goes wrong.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Isolate your test data">
    Create test sub-accounts with a recognisable `reference` prefix such as `test-`, and use the same prefix on withdrawal references. Test records are then trivial to find and exclude from reporting.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Keep it behind a flag">
    Restrict the integration to internal users or a feature flag until every check below passes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The controlled production test

Run this end to end before opening the flow to any real cohort.

| # | Action | Pass criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Access and setup | The key works, is IP-restricted, and the webhook endpoint is reachable and verifying signatures. |
| 1 | Create a sub-account | `200`. The returned `id` is stored against your user, and your `reference` comes back on later payloads. |
| 2 | Fetch the wallet | `blockchain_enabled`, `default_network` and `networks` are read correctly. Your network picker reflects `deposits_enabled` and `withdraws_enabled`, not a hard-coded list. |
| 3 | Generate an address | An address returns, or `wallet.address.generated` arrives. The network and `destination_tag` are displayed alongside it. |
| 4 | Send a small deposit | `deposit.transaction.confirmation` then `deposit.successful` arrive. **The customer is not credited at this stage.** |
| 5 | Re-query the deposit | Status is `ACCEPTED` before any value is given. The credit happens exactly once, keyed on the deposit ID. |
| 6 | Replay the same webhook | Sending the identical event again credits nothing further. Idempotency holds. |
| 7 | Sweep internally | An internal transfer to your parent account settles instantly, with `fee` of zero and `type: internal_transfer`. |
| 8 | Withdraw on-chain | The withdrawal completes, `withdraw.successful` arrives, and `txId` matches what you see on-chain. |
| 9 | Reconcile | `amount`, `fee` and `total` match across the API response, the webhook, the dashboard and your own ledger. |
| 10 | Look up by reference | `GET /users/{user_id}/withdraws/reference/{reference}` returns the withdrawal you created. |
| 11 | Exercise a rejection | A withdrawal to a deliberately invalid destination is rejected, `reason` is recorded, and your escalation path fires. |
| 12 | Exercise the rate limit | Your client backs off correctly on `444` rather than treating it as a connection error. |
| 13 | Open limited traffic | Only after every check above passes. Monitor errors, latency, webhook delivery, balances and reconciliation before increasing. |

<Warning>
  **Stop conditions.** Do not open public traffic if any of these is true:

  - The production API key is unavailable, unrestricted, or not working.
  - A deposit does not reach `ACCEPTED`, or you cannot tell when it has.
  - The `deposit.successful` or `withdraw.successful` webhook is not received.
  - A replayed webhook credits a customer twice.
  - The dashboard and your ledger do not reconcile.
  - You cannot guarantee a customer is credited exactly once.
  - Your client treats `444` as a hard failure rather than backing off.
</Warning>

## What to test without spending anything

Several failure paths cost nothing to exercise, and they are where most launch issues hide.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Validation errors" icon="triangle-alert">
    Submit a bad currency, a malformed amount, a missing reference, and a non-existent sub-account. Confirm you read `data.code` rather than parsing `message`.

    <Card title="Error codes" icon="triangle-alert" href="/wallets/errors#error-codes" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Authentication failures" icon="key">
    Call with no key, a wrong key, and over plain HTTP. Confirm you handle `401`, `E0001` and the `301` from a non-TLS request — and that a key sent over HTTP gets rotated.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Duplicate references" icon="fingerprint">
    Submit the same withdrawal `reference` twice. Confirm your code treats the rejection as "already sent" rather than retrying with a new reference and paying twice.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Webhook signature failures" icon="shield-check">
    Replay a payload with a tampered body and confirm you reject it. Then replay one with a valid signature but an old timestamp.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Trade-only assets" icon="ban">
    Try to generate an address for an asset that cannot be received. Confirm your interface never offers a deposit flow for one.

    <Card title="Coverage and limits" icon="globe" href="/wallets/coverage" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Precision and minimums" icon="ruler">
    Submit an order below the minimum and one with too many decimal places. Confirm you round and validate before calling rather than surfacing a raw rejection.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Before you launch

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Keys are restricted and server-side" icon="lock">
    IP-restricted where possible, never in client code, never committed, never logged. You have a rotation procedure you have actually run.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Webhooks are verified and idempotent" icon="webhook">
    Signature checked with a timing-safe comparison, `200` returned before processing, every event keyed and deduplicated.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="You alert on webhook failures" icon="bell">
    Retries stop permanently after 24 hours and cannot be replayed. A silent outage means silently lost events, and the only recovery is reconciling against the API.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Balances are read correctly" icon="scale-balanced">
    Spendable balance is `balance` alone, never `balance + locked` or `+ staked`.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Networks are driven off the API" icon="link">
    Address generation always passes an explicit `network`, and your picker reflects the live `deposits_enabled` and `withdraws_enabled` flags.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reconciliation runs on a schedule" icon="list-check">
    A periodic job compares your ledger against the API, so a missed webhook surfaces within hours rather than at month end.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Support has a runbook" icon="life-ring">
    Your team knows what `ON_HOLD` means, what `FAILED_AML` means, that on-chain withdrawals cannot be reversed, and what to send support — reference, timestamp with timezone, request and response.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
