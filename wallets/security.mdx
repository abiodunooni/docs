The Wallets secret key is the most sensitive credential Quidax issues. It reaches **every sub-account in your tree** and it can withdraw funds. There is no per-sub-account credential and no read-only mode, so treat it as the key to the whole estate.

## The controls that matter

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Whitelist your IPs — up to 10" icon="filter">
    Bind the key to your egress addresses when you create it. A leaked key that only works from your infrastructure is close to worthless to an attacker.

    Route outbound traffic through a fixed NAT gateway and whitelist that, rather than tracking autoscaled instance IPs.

    <Card title="IP whitelisting" icon="filter" href="/wallets/authentication#ip-whitelisting" horizontal />
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Server-side only" icon="lock">
    Never in browser or mobile code, never in a repository, never in logs or error reports. A key in a mobile binary is a key in an attacker's hands.

    Scrub `Authorization` headers from your logging and error tracking explicitly — most frameworks will happily serialise them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Always HTTPS" icon="shield-check">
    A plain HTTP request returns `301` and sends your key in the clear on the way. Any key that has travelled over HTTP is compromised — rotate it rather than assuming nobody was listening.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Have a rotation procedure you have run" icon="arrows-rotate">
    Not a document. An actual procedure someone has executed end to end, ideally in the last quarter.

    The moment you need to rotate is the worst possible moment to discover the key is hard-coded in three services.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Verify every webhook signature" icon="fingerprint">
    Your webhook URL is public and unauthenticated. Without signature verification, anyone who finds it can tell you a deposit succeeded.

    HMAC SHA256 over `<timestamp>.<body>`, compared with a timing-safe comparison.

    <Card title="Webhooks" icon="webhook" href="/wallets/webhooks#verifying-a-request" horizontal />
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Rate limits as a safety net

| Endpoint | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Address generation | 20 per second |
| Everything else | 300 per minute |

Over the limit returns `444`. Beyond protecting the platform, a sudden rate-limit spike in your own metrics is a useful early signal that something is looping or someone is probing.

## Designing for the blast radius

Because one key does everything, the interesting question is what happens if it leaks.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Keep hot balances small" icon="vault">
    Sweep customer deposits to a treasury account, and keep only what you need for expected payouts in the account the key touches most.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Approve large payouts out of band" icon="user-check">
    Require a human step above a threshold you choose. An automated payout pipeline with no ceiling is an automated exfiltration pipeline if the key leaks.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Alert on unusual withdrawal patterns" icon="bell">
    Volume, frequency, and new destination addresses. You will see an anomaly in your own metrics long before anyone reports one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reconcile on a schedule" icon="list-check">
    A job comparing your ledger against the API surfaces both a missed webhook and an unexpected movement, within hours rather than at month end.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Irreversibility

Blockchain movements cannot be undone. There is no chargeback, no recall and no support path that reverses a confirmed on-chain withdrawal.

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong address | Validate format client-side; test-send to new large destinations |
| Wrong network | Always pass an explicit `network`; check `withdraws_enabled` |
| Duplicate payment | Pre-generated unique references; look up before resubmitting |
| Compromised key | IP whitelist; small hot balances; rotation procedure |

<Warning>
  The 6-second cancellation window is not a safety control. It catches a programmatic mistake in the moment, not a bad address discovered a minute later.
</Warning>
